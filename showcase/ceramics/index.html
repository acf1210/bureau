Five joints, five decisions.

A potter talks about a pot the way a tailor talks about a jacket. The foot decides how it sits, the belly how much it holds, the shoulder where the eye stops, the neck how it pours, and the lip how it feels against a mouth.

We trim every foot by hand and leave it unglazed, so you can see the clay it was made from.

Twenty-two hours in the kiln.

The glaze only becomes glass above 1,240 °C. We climb slowly through the first six hundred degrees, when water still hides in the clay, hold at the top, and let it cool for as long as it took to heat.
