MaréOcean Institute, Horta, Azores

We measure the Atlantic from the surface to 4,000 m: its waves, its tide, and the light that stops on the way down. This is the sea as our instruments see it, today.

0–200metres

Sunlight

Everything that eats, eventually, eats from here. Plankton turn sunlight into the sea's first food, and our moored buoys count them by the colour they leave in the water.

200–1,000metres

Twilight

Red is the first colour to go and blue the last. At night, billions of animals rise from here to feed at the surface and sink again before dawn: the largest migration on Earth, every day.

1,000–4,000metres

Midnight

No sunlight at all. The only light is made by the animals themselves. Our landers sit on the slope for six months at a time and photograph whatever comes to look at them.

4,000metres, the floor

The floor

Two degrees, four hundred atmospheres, and a slow snow of everything that died above. A sediment core from here holds a hundred thousand years in a metre of mud.
