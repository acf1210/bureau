Engagement letter, clauses 1 to 4

  1. The Chambers shall represent the Client in the arbitration described in Schedule A before the constituted under the rules of the seat.

    We represent you in this one arbitration, in front of the people the rules appoint to decide it.

  2. Fees accrue at the rates in Schedule B, invoiced monthly, and are capped at the unless the Client consents in writing to its revision.

    You get a bill every month, and it never goes above the agreed maximum unless you sign off first.

  3. The Chambers shall not accept any engagement adverse to the Client that arises from the same facts for the duration of the matter and two years thereafter.

    For the case and two years after it, we will not act against you on anything connected to it.

  4. Either party may terminate on thirty days' notice; work in progress at the shall be completed or handed over at the Client's election.

    Either of us can end this with a month's notice. You decide whether we finish open work or hand it over.

Four phases, and who owns each one.

The rules of the seat set the calendar. What we control is that you always know which phase you are in and what is expected of you in it.

  1. Request

    We draft the request for arbitration and the first statement of the claim, from your documents.

    You: documents and one interview.

  2. Constitution

    The tribunal is appointed. We propose arbitrators and check every candidate for conflicts.

    You: approve the nominee.

  3. Written phase

    Statements, evidence and expert reports are exchanged in rounds the tribunal sets.

    You: witnesses and review.

  4. Hearing and award

    We present the case. The tribunal deliberates and issues a written, reasoned award.

    You: attend the hearing.

Three kinds of dispute, nothing else.

A narrow practice is a deliberate one. If your matter is outside these, we will say so in the conflict check and name someone better placed.
