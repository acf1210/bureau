Set the waterline

Choose a rise scenario in centimetres. The desk translates it into a year, a level and a set of exposed assets.

Read the order of failure

Infrastructure fails in sequence. The model ranks assets by the level at which they stop serving their function, not by replacement cost.

Choose the adaptation window

Every scenario produces a window, not a verdict. The output is a briefing, a set of trade-offs and the first three moves.

The questions planners actually ask.

Short answers, with the assumptions left visible.

Is this a flood map?

No. It is a decision surface for comparing scenarios. A flood map answers where water goes; this desk answers what stops working first, and in what order.

Where does the data come from?

This template ships with an illustrative dataset. Production deployments connect to a licensed model, a tide gauge feed and the municipality's asset register.

Can it run offline?

Yes. The interface is static and the model can be compiled to a local dataset. That matters for councils with restricted networks and for field reviews.

What does the output look like?

A short briefing: the scenario, the exposed assets in failure order, the adaptation window, three moves and the assumptions that would change the answer.
