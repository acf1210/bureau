# 32 — Visual style vocabulary

Use these styles as lenses for art direction, not templates or predictions of business results. This vocabulary is informed by [Tilda Education's guide to popular web design styles](https://pt-br.tilda.education/en/web-design-styles), accessed 2026-10-27. Its discussion of 2025 trends is historical; verify contemporary examples before claiming a style is current.

**Realism is not a style on this list.** It is the default material underneath all of them. A minimal page can be photographic; a brutalist page can use documentary images; a flat interface still needs real product photography where the product is shown. None of these lenses authorises a vector hero or an abstract object in place of a subject that exists in the world. See `39-realism-engine.md`.

## Choose from the brief

Start with the audience's task, content, brand truth and constraints. For each direction, identify the visual principle, the specific need it serves and the trade-off. Combine influences across different axes rather than copying a site's composition or adopting an aesthetic for its own sake. Test the resulting direction with real content, at mobile and desktop sizes, with keyboard and reduced motion.

| Lens | Useful visual moves | When it can help | Risk to check |
|---|---|---|
| Minimal | Restrained palette, generous negative space, few deliberate elements | A small amount of content needs focused attention | Sparse pages can hide hierarchy, context or affordances. |
| Brutalist / neobrutalist | Large type, raw geometry, assertive contrast and unexpected arrangement | An expressive subject can support deliberate tension | Roughness is not an excuse for poor contrast, broken navigation or heavy borders on every card. |
| Constructivist | Geometric forms, asymmetry, strong direction and collage | A subject benefits from a sense of movement or a clear graphic argument | Movement and overlapping shapes must not interrupt reading or control access. |
| Swiss | Modular alignment, typographic hierarchy and functional images | Complex information needs order and repeatable structure | A precise grid without a subject-specific voice can feel interchangeable. |
| Editorial | Contrast in type, authored pacing, images, captions and varied compositions | A story or publication needs a reading journey | Large images and display type must not compromise measure, contrast or loading. |
| Hand-drawn | Purposeful marks, illustration, irregular edges and crafted texture | The subject has a credible human or handmade character | Keep body text readable; confirm rights to artwork and avoid decorative clutter. |
| Retro | Materials, type and visual cues grounded in a documented period | A project has a meaningful historical or cultural connection | Nostalgia can become generic; preserve modern accessibility and avoid unlicensed imitation. |
| Flat | Solid color, simple forms, clear labels and restrained depth | A task surface benefits from fast recognition | Controls still need visible boundaries, states and sufficient contrast. |
| Bento | Differently sized grouped regions for distinct content | A dashboard or overview has genuinely separate units | Do not default to rounded cards or force unrelated material into equal boxes. |

## Turn a lens into a design

1. Write a one-sentence rationale connecting the lens to the brief and an explicit rejection of a plausible alternative.
2. Identify a dominant, counter and accent influence with source and axis; document what must not be copied. A named style is not a substitute for a lineage.
3. Sketch three distinct directions with the same real content. Specify hierarchy, type, palette, image treatment, spacing, behavior and the nonanimated state.
4. Review the chosen composition for accessible contrast, semantics, readable type, keyboard use, mobile reflow, reduced motion, asset rights and performance. Apply BUREAU's anti-slop and review rules even when a style commonly uses effects they prohibit.
5. Record the decision, evidence and unresolved risks in the project's existing design and review artifacts. Test claims about usability or conversion with relevant evidence rather than inferring them from style.
